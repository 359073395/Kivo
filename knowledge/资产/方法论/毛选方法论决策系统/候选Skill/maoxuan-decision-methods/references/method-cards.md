# Candidate method cards

Each card is a decision component, not a separate Skill.

## M01 Investigation before judgment

- Trigger: the decision depends on claims that are old, second-hand, vague or disputed.
- Do: define the question; collect present facts and relevant history; record source, time and exceptions; separate observation from explanation; map affected stakeholders by observable interests, constraints and decision rights rather than historical “enemy/friend” labels.
- Check: could another person reproduce the evidence table?
- Stop: when the decisive unknowns fall below the predeclared threshold, or when further investigation costs more than the reversible test.
- Failure: collecting material without changing any decision variable.

## M02 Practice–knowledge loop

- Trigger: a plausible idea lacks proof in the current setting.
- Do: induce a candidate pattern from observations; check sampling and counterexamples; derive an observable prediction from the candidate and its premises; run the smallest safe test; compare expected with actual; revise the premises and conclusion with new evidence.
- Check: prediction and alternative explanations were written before the result.
- Stop: safety breach, invalid measurement, or a predeclared failure threshold.
- Failure: renaming every unexpected result a “new stage” after the fact.

## M03 Anti-dogmatism and anti-empiricism

- Trigger: someone wants to copy a theory, competitor or prior success directly.
- Do: compare source conditions with current conditions; separate the proposed general mechanism from this domain's special constraints; list what is invariant and what changed; seek an independent counterexample. Before execution, require all four inputs: a general frame, current domain knowledge, an executable playbook and investigated case facts.
- Check: the recommendation is neither “the book says so” nor “it worked once.”
- Stop: if no transferable mechanism remains after condition matching.

## M04 Contradiction and priority diagnosis

- Trigger: many problems compete for limited attention.
- Do: define each tension as two interacting sides; classify its nature; list candidate main contradictions; score impact, urgency, controllability, evidence and dependency; choose one current priority.
- Check: explain why the runner-up is not first and what signal would reverse the ranking.
- Stop: hard safety/legal/cash-flow gate fails, or evidence cannot distinguish candidates.
- Failure: using “main contradiction” to erase secondary but catastrophic risks.

## M05 Stage and strategy

- Trigger: the objective cannot be achieved in one step or conditions are changing.
- Do: compare own capability, counterpart/stakeholder behavior, resources, time and external constraints; use trend, real demand and own advantage only as course-layer questions, then add competition, legality, delivery and cash-flow evidence; identify a controllable missing condition; distinguish global constraints from a reversible local test; define stages, center task and observable transition signals.
- Check: every stage has a different purpose, entry condition and exit condition.
- Stop: the original objective becomes invalid, costs exceed the ceiling, or the stage signal fails repeatedly.
- Failure: “long term” without review dates or loss limits.

## M06 Individual pilot and front-line loop

- Trigger: a general policy or content idea needs operational proof.
- Do: select a small representative pilot; predeclare its success threshold; gather user/front-line observations; synthesize a concrete version; return it to the same front line for test; repeat enough to distinguish a pattern from one lucky result; then scale with a cap and watch for effect decay.
- Check: record selection bias and whether the pilot represents the target population.
- Stop: pilot harm, no reliable feedback, evidence that scaling conditions differ, or effect decay beyond the predeclared limit.
- Failure: announcing a general goal without a named owner or treating loud voices as the whole population.

## M07 Collaboration with autonomy

- Trigger: external partners, platforms, creators or tools can accelerate the objective.
- Do: establish contact, mutual understanding and reciprocal value without assuming trust; define common objective, non-negotiable boundaries, contribution, decision rights, data/asset ownership, review cadence and exit plan; use the M01 stakeholder map to distinguish supporters, neutrals and blockers without dehumanizing labels.
- Check: the user retains core data, relationships, know-how and a viable fallback.
- Stop: boundary breach, asymmetric dependence beyond the ceiling, or loss of auditability.
- Failure: confusing cooperation with surrender of control, or self-reliance with refusing useful aid.

## M08 Concentrate limited resources

- Trigger: the main contradiction is identified but resources are spread thin.
- Do: reserve hard-gate capacity; allocate a capped majority to the decisive bottleneck; test one measurable breakthrough point, expand only after the threshold repeats, and freeze low-value side projects for the observation window.
- Check: the allocation follows evidence and is reversible where possible.
- Stop: bottleneck diagnosis disproved, leading indicator deteriorates past the limit, or concentration creates unacceptable systemic risk.
- Failure: copying military language/actions or betting everything with no reserve.

## M09 Corrective review

- Trigger: an action misses, outperforms or produces an unexpected side effect.
- Do: compare expected versus actual; separate diagnosis, execution, measurement and environment; invite disconfirming front-line evidence; record a candidate lesson and next test.
- Check: no blame label substitutes for causal evidence.
- Stop: once the next falsifiable change is defined, or the project itself meets a stop condition.
- Failure: self-criticism as humiliation, political ritual, or retroactive certainty.

## Course-tool appendix: option generation

Source level: `解读/课程观点`, not an original-text method card.

When the fact sheet and hard gates are already clear but there is only one apparent option, generate alternatives by: keeping the objective while reversing one assumption; combining compatible parts of two options; changing sequence, scale or owner; and adding a safe no-action option. Every generated option must still pass current evidence, legality, safety, cash-flow and domain checks. Divergence creates candidates; it does not prove that novelty is useful.
